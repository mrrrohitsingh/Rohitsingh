    What lawyers and advocates have done for India

    A student’s overview of a few examples. Read the full judgments on official court websites before quoting them.

    • Freedom and nation building: many leaders of the freedom movement were lawyers, including Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and Dr. Rajendra Prasad. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, trained in law, chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constitution.
    • Protecting the Constitution: in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) the Supreme Court laid down the basic structure doctrine. Senior advocate Nani Palkhivala argued for the petitioners.
    • Life, liberty and justice for the poor: Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) gave Article 21 a wide meaning, and Hussainara Khatoon (1979) drew attention to undertrial prisoners kept in jail for long periods. Cases like these helped shape public interest litigation.
    • Free speech online: in Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015) the Supreme Court struck down Section 66A of the IT Act. The petitioner was a law student at the time.
    • Privacy: K. S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017) recognised privacy as a fundamental right.
    • Environment: M. C. Mehta, an environmental lawyer, brought many cases that led to court orders on pollution, including the river Ganga and Delhi’s air.
